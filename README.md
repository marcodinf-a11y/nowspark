# NowSpark

**One small action starts the spark.**

An open-source, Android-first helper for organizing yourself and making progress toward your goals, one small step at a time, with a desktop companion for when you're at your computer.

**Status:** Concept stage; no app is available yet. Android is the primary platform, with a desktop companion planned.

[Public repository](https://github.com/marcodinf-a11y/nowspark)

## How NowSpark aims to help

NowSpark should support the whole journey from intention to completion: help you understand what is making progress difficult, choose a manageable next action, and adjust the workload as life happens. The aim is to reduce procrastination day by day and help prevent work from building into a crushing wave near a deadline.

- **Understand the obstacle:** Help the user reflect on what is getting in the way, such as an unclear task, an uncomfortable step, competing demands, or an unrealistic plan.
- **Make the next step doable:** Turn a goal into a concrete action that fits the user's available time and energy.
- **Stay involved through completion:** Check what happened, recognize progress, and help recover when a step stalls.
- **Spread work over time:** Revisit remaining work and available capacity early enough to change the plan, reduce scope, or seek help.
- **Supportive accountability:** Offer a nudge toward action with a level of directness the user controls. Make room for deliberate rest, rescheduling, and changing a goal.
- **Phone first:** The main experience belongs on Android, always within reach.
- **Desktop companion:** Support the same purpose at your desk; its exact role is still to be defined.
- **Open source:** Develop in public under the MIT license.

## Starting with everyday tasks

**Initial focus: everyday tasks that pile up.** Deadline-driven projects and personal goals without firm deadlines remain part of the longer-term vision.

Start with one useful loop:

1. Capture an everyday task that is weighing on you, without needing to organize the entire backlog first.
2. Choose one next action small enough to fit the time and energy available now.
3. Try a short, optional focus session.
4. Record what happened and what got in the way, then continue, shrink the step, or take a break.
5. Revisit what remains, account for anything time-sensitive, and choose a realistic next opportunity to continue.

For example, "deal with the pile of letters" might become "open one envelope and check whether it needs a response." The app should help the user identify the next action and any due date, then make it easy to return later. Step size should adapt to the user and task.

These are starting ideas to validate through personal use, rather than a fixed feature list.

## Guiding principles

- Make starting take less effort than organizing.
- Offer encouragement without guilt or punishment for missed days.
- Keep personal entries private and under the user's control.
- Make the app useful even when motivation is low.
- Treat missed steps as information for adjusting the plan.
- Keep planning lightweight enough that it helps action instead of becoming another task to avoid.
- Ground design choices in research, while treating the app's effectiveness as something to test through use.

See the [behavioral research foundations](research/nowspark-behavioral-foundations.md) for initial evidence, limitations, and design hypotheses.
